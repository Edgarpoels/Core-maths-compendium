Homework · a practical guide for teachers

Setting weekly homework for a year-long Core Maths course

Core Maths runs a full academic year with no coursework and one shot at the exam, so consistent weekly homework matters — but so does not drowning in marking. This page compares Google Forms, Google Classroom's native tools, the free AMSP/Integral Core Maths platform, Dr Frost Maths, paid platforms like Sparx and Eedi, and the plain worksheet-plus-answer-key approach, then sets out a rotating weekly system that keeps marking light without losing the exam-style written practice Core Maths actually needs.

What the evidence says

Four principles worth building the system around

Drawn from the Education Endowment Foundation's homework research and Unity Research School's review of maths-specific homework studies.

1

Short and frequent beats long and rare

The homework studies with the strongest impact set work twice a week rather than once. A focused 10–15 minute task little and often outperforms an hour-long task set weekly.

2

Reinforce, don't introduce

Homework should practise what's already been taught, not deliver new content cold. Tasks that mirror the examples used in the lesson give pupils a solid foundation to build on.

3

Keep the time load bounded

Roughly two hours a week of homework across a subject is well supported by evidence; four hours isn't — more time does not reliably mean more progress.

4

Feedback quality > volume

Homework linked clearly to what was just taught, with fast and specific feedback, is what drives the gains — which is exactly what auto-marking is good for.

The recommended system

A three-week rotation, repeated all year

Rather than picking one tool, rotate three short, purposeful homework types. Each is auto-marked or self-marked, so your job most weeks is reviewing a results summary rather than marking scripts.

Week 1 · Retrieval

Google Form quiz

8–10 auto-marked questions mixing this week's topic with two or three from earlier chapters. Build one template once, duplicate it weekly, and post it as a Classroom assignment.

~10 minutes for pupils · ~5 minutes for you to skim the results sheet

Week 2 · Practice

Integral section test

An auto-marked section test on the Core Maths platform, tied directly to the topic just taught. Use the grader report to spot which questions the class found hardest and reteach five minutes of it.

~15 minutes for pupils · near-zero marking

Week 3 · Exam technique

Worksheet, self-marked

One of this site's worksheets or end-of-topic test questions, done on paper with full working. Pupils self-mark against the answer key in the next lesson; collect books every third cycle to spot-check.

~20 minutes for pupils · spot-check only, not full marking

Why keep the worksheet week — Core Maths is assessed entirely through written exams that demand full working and interpretation, not just a final number. Auto-marked tools are excellent for retrieval and fluency, but they can't substitute for the multi-step, exam-style writing practice a worksheet week gives pupils.

Getting set up

Five things to do before week one

1
Register your school with the AMSP (free) to unlock the Integral Core Maths platform's auto-marked section tests
2
Build one reusable Google Form template with 8–10 mixed-recall questions you can duplicate every retrieval week
3
Create a Google Classroom topic per chapter and post each week's homework there on a consistent day
4
Print or share this site's worksheets and end-of-topic tests, each with its own answer key, for exam-technique weeks
5
Every half-term, have pupils update the Course Tracker so homework results and topic mastery show up side by side
